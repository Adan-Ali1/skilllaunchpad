A PRACTICAL STARTING POINT

Free Digital Skills Learning Resources

You do not need to collect courses before you begin. Pick one skill, use one reliable learning path, and make a small project that shows what you practised. These official learning libraries are useful starting points for a worldwide beginner audience.

Choose a path that fits the work

  • Coding and web development: freeCodeCamp Learn ↗ offers a self-paced curriculum for programming and web development. Start with a beginner section and complete small exercises rather than only watching tutorials.
  • Technology and software skills: Microsoft Learn ↗ has self-directed modules and learning paths across Microsoft technologies. Use its topic filters to choose a focused beginner lesson.
  • Marketing and business skills: HubSpot Academy ↗ provides online learning in marketing, sales, service, and related topics. Its site describes its training library as free; check account and course requirements before starting.
  • Google product skills: Google Skillshop ↗ is Google's training platform for its products and solutions. Course access, language options, and certification terms may vary, so review each course page.

Turn a lesson into a portfolio sample

After a short module, make something original that demonstrates the skill: a sample landing page, a fictional campaign plan, a practice spreadsheet, or a simple design. Label practice work clearly; never present a fictional brief as paid client work or invent results.

Use the free Portfolio Brief Builder to choose a practice project. Then follow the portfolio guide to explain your process honestly.

A simple seven-day learning routine

  1. Day 1: Choose one skill and a small task you want to do.
  2. Days 2–3: Complete a few lessons from one provider and note unfamiliar terms.
  3. Days 4–5: Repeat the task without copying each tutorial step.
  4. Day 6: Ask for specific feedback about whether your result is clear and usable.
  5. Day 7: Improve one thing and write an honest note describing the sample as practice.

This is a learning routine, not a promise of a job, income, certificate, or fixed skill level.

Check details before you commit

“Free training” does not always mean every certificate, exam, tool, or internet cost is free. Providers can change course requirements, languages, and regional access. Confirm those details on their official pages. You do not need a certificate for every kind of work; a relevant sample can help show what you can do.
